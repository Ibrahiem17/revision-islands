🎯 Common DevOps Behavioral Questions + How to Approach
B01Tell me about a time you automated a tedious manual process.▼
HOW TO ANSWER
What they want to hear: You proactively identified inefficiency, built a solution, and measured the impact.

Structure (STAR):
• Situation: "Deployments required 12 manual steps and took 2 hours each, with frequent human errors."
• Task: "I needed to make this repeatable and error-free."
• Action: "I built a GitHub Actions pipeline that automated the entire process — tests, Docker build, push to ECR, and kubectl apply — triggered on every merge to main."
• Result: "Deployment time dropped from 2 hours to 8 minutes. Zero manual errors since. The team could ship 3x more frequently."

Tip: Relate this to your actual AlphaCRM or URL Shortener projects — you built CI/CD pipelines with Docker and GitHub Actions.
B02How do you handle developers pushing back on security or infrastructure constraints?▼
HOW TO ANSWER
What they want to hear: You can collaborate without being a blocker, you explain the "why" not just the "no", and you find solutions not just problems.

Key points to hit:
• Understand their perspective first — why is the constraint painful for them?
• Explain the risk in plain terms, not just policy compliance: "This would expose customer PII to anyone who gets the access token."
• Offer alternatives: "We can't open port 22 to 0.0.0.0/0, but we can set up a bastion host or use AWS SSM Session Manager — same access, no exposed port."
• DevOps is a bridge role — security and developer velocity are both your responsibility, not opposites.

Avoid: "I just said no and escalated." That's not collaboration.
B03Describe a time you caused an outage or made a major mistake. How did you fix it and what did you learn?▼
HOW TO ANSWER
What they want to hear: You take ownership (no blame-shifting), you responded effectively, and you turned it into a learning moment with systemic improvements.

Structure:
• Be honest — don't say "I've never made a mistake." Everyone has. Refusing to share is a red flag.
• Describe the mistake clearly — what happened, what the impact was.
• What you did immediately — how did you communicate and recover?
• What you changed afterwards — what process/safeguard did you add so it can't happen again? This is the most important part.

Example angle: "I pushed a config change to production without testing it in staging first. The app went down for 20 minutes. I immediately rolled back. Afterwards, I added a staging environment requirement to the CI/CD pipeline — no changes can bypass staging anymore."

Key message: You made it structurally impossible to repeat the mistake.
B04A developer complains their application is running slowly on a Linux server. What do you run?▼
HOW TO ANSWER
Systematic approach — don't guess, measure:

CPU: top or htop — is the app maxing out CPU? Any other processes stealing CPU?
Memory: free -h — is RAM full? Is the system swapping to disk? (Swap = very slow)
Disk I/O: iostat -x 1 or iotop — is the disk saturated? High iowait in top indicates disk bottleneck.
Disk space: df -h — full disk can cause slow writes and crashes.
Network: netstat -s or ss -s — retransmit errors, connection states.
Application logs: Slow DB queries? External API timeouts? tail -f app.log
System errors: dmesg | tail — kernel-level errors?

top → free → df → iostat → logsdon't guess, measure first
B05A deployment just failed in production. Walk me through your immediate next steps.▼
HOW TO ANSWER
Step 1 — Assess scope FAST: Is it a partial failure or complete outage? Are all users affected or some?

Step 2 — Rollback FIRST, investigate later: If users are impacted, don't spend 30 minutes debugging — rollback immediately to the last known good version. In Kubernetes: kubectl rollout undo deployment/myapp. In ECS: redeploy the previous task definition. Restore service first.

Step 3 — Communicate: Immediately notify your team and stakeholders: "Deployment X failed, rolling back, ETA 5 minutes." Update every 15 minutes.

Step 4 — Investigate safely: Once users are restored, investigate in a non-prod environment. Look at pipeline logs, application logs, diff the config between old and new versions.

Step 5 — Post-mortem: After fix, blameless post-mortem. What failed? How do we detect it earlier next time? What safeguard do we add?

📝 What is a Build Artifact?
A build artifact is the deployable output generated by the build stage of a CI/CD pipeline. It's the final packaged product that moves forward through testing and deployment stages.

Examples: A compiled Java .jar file, a Docker image, a zipped Node.js app, a compiled Go binary, a bundled React app.

Why it matters: Artifacts are built once and promoted through environments (dev → staging → prod). You test the exact same artifact — not "build again in each environment." This ensures what you tested is exactly what you deployed.
